How to read Obsidian notes on Android without Obsidian

Updated: 2026-11-27

You can read Obsidian notes on Android without installing Obsidian, because every note is an ordinary .md file. Copy the notes to your phone and open them in any markdown reader; headings, lists, tables and checklists show up formatted.

Obsidian does have its own Android app, and if you want your full setup on the phone, that's the natural choice. But sometimes you only want to look at a few notes, read them on a phone that isn't yours, or keep reading simple. This guide covers that case.

Why your notes open without Obsidian

Obsidian keeps everything in a vault, which its help pages describe as a folder on your computer, including its subfolders. Each note inside is a plain text file written in markdown, a simple way of marking headings and lists with symbols like # and -. (New to the format? See what a .md file is.)

So there's no special database to unlock. Any app that understands markdown can read the notes. The vault also contains a hidden .obsidian folder with Obsidian's settings; you can ignore it.

Get the vault onto your phone

Pick whatever is easiest for you:

  • Google Drive: upload the vault folder, or just the notes you need, then open them from the Drive app. Our guide to opening .md files from Google Drive shows the steps.
  • Email: attach one or two notes and send them to yourself.
  • USB cable: copy the folder to your phone's storage or the Downloads folder.

If your vault is already synced to the phone by some other method, the files are already there. Look for them in Files (or My Files on Samsung).

Read a single note or the whole vault

To read one note, tap the .md file and choose MD Reader, or open the app and use Open file.

The note appears formatted. Contents lists its headings so you can jump around a long note, and the app reopens each document where you left off. It works without internet; MD Reader has no internet permission at all, so your notes stay on the device.

For a whole vault, MD Reader Pro (a one-time purchase) adds Open folder. You open the vault folder once, search across all of its notes, and follow links from one note to another. Reading single notes is free.

What looks different outside Obsidian

Most of what you write in Obsidian is standard markdown, and it shows up the same. Some things are worth knowing:

  • Callouts such as > [!NOTE] appear as highlighted boxes, as they do in Obsidian.
  • Front matter, the block of settings between --- lines at the top of a note, is shown as a small card instead of raw text.
  • Checklists, tables, footnotes, math and Mermaid diagrams are rendered, with no internet needed.
  • Wikilinks like [[Meeting notes]] are Obsidian's default way to link notes, and they aren't standard markdown, so other apps may not treat them as links. Obsidian can write standard links instead: in its settings, under Files and links, turn off Use [[Wikilinks]].
  • Plugin output is created by Obsidian while it runs. Outside Obsidian you see the note as it's written, not what a plugin would add.
  • Images that point to web addresses won't load, because the app never goes online.

Editing: what changes where

You can fix a typo or tick a box on the phone. Tap Edit and type on the formatted page, without needing to know the symbols (more on editing). Checkboxes can be ticked while reading and are saved right away.

Keep in mind that MD Reader doesn't sync anything. If you copied the note to your phone, you're editing that copy, and your computer's vault won't see the change until you send the file back. If you plan to edit a lot on the go, keep one place as the main copy so you don't end up with two versions of the same note.
