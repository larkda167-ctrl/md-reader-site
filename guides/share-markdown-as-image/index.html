How to share a markdown document as images on Android

Updated: 2026-11-24

To share markdown as an image, open the document in an app that displays it formatted, then export the formatted page as pictures instead of sending the raw text. The person you send it to sees clean headings, tables and lists, without needing any special app.

This matters because most chat apps don't understand markdown. Markdown is the plain-text format AI assistants and many note apps use, where # marks a heading and ** makes text bold. Paste it into a group chat and your friends get a wall of symbols. An image looks the same on every phone.

Why send markdown as images

Images solve a few common problems at once:

  • The formatting survives. Tables, checklists and code blocks look the way you see them, not as | and - characters.
  • Nobody needs to install anything. Every messenger can show a picture.
  • It reads well in a chat. People scroll through images more easily than through a long pasted message or a file they have to open.

The trade-off is that text in an image can't be copied or searched by the person who receives it. If they need to reuse the words, send text or a file instead (more on that below).

How to share a document as images

On Android, MD Reader can do this in a few taps:

  1. Open the document. Tap a .md file and choose MD Reader, or use Open file in the app. For an answer you copied from ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, use Paste as document.
  2. Open the menu (⋮) and tap Share, then Share as images.
  3. You'll see the document split into pages, shown as thumbnails. Tap the pages you want, or use Select all or Clear.
  4. Tap Share and pick the app to send them with, such as KakaoTalk or WhatsApp. The selected pages go out together as several images.

How pages are split

MD Reader cuts the document into screen-sized pages, so each image fits comfortably on a phone. It never cuts in the middle of a paragraph, so no sentence is split between two pictures.

This is why picking pages is useful. If only the summary at the top and one table matter, select those two and leave out the rest. The other person gets exactly what they need, not a long scroll.

What's free and what needs Pro

Sharing as images is free, with two limits: up to 5 pages at a time, and a small "MD Reader" mark on each image. MD Reader Pro, a one-time purchase on Google Play, removes both, so you can send any number of pages without the mark.

If you only need a few pages now and then, the free version is enough. For longer documents you can also send them in batches of five.

When text or PDF is better

Images aren't always the right choice:

  • Share as text sends the markdown itself. Use it when the other person will edit the content or paste it into another app that understands markdown.
  • A PDF keeps everything in one file that can be printed or attached to an email. In MD Reader, Print / Save as PDF is a Pro feature. See how to convert markdown to PDF on Android.
  • The file itself is best for anyone who will keep working on it on a computer.

Things to check before you send

  • Pictures from the web won't appear. MD Reader has no internet permission, so images in the document that point to web addresses don't load and won't be in the shared images either. Pictures that only link to a website are best described in words.
  • Check the thumbnails. Look through the selected pages once before you tap Share, so nothing is missing or included by mistake.
  • Personal details. An image is easy to forward and hard to take back. Look through the pages for names, phone numbers or addresses you didn't mean to share.
