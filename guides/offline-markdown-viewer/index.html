Offline markdown viewer for Android: why "no internet permission" matters

Updated: 2026-10-29

Markdown files are often personal: journal entries, work notes, meeting minutes, health logs, AI answers about private questions. Before you open them in an app, it's worth asking one question: can this app send my files anywhere?

The quickest check: the Internet permission

On Android, an app needs the Internet permission to connect to any server. Without it, it physically cannot upload anything: not your files, not analytics, not ads.

To check an app:

  1. On its Google Play page, scroll to About this app and look for App permissions (you may need to tap See more).
  2. Or, on your phone, go to Settings → Apps → [the app] → Permissions → All permissions (wording varies by phone).
  3. Look for "have full network access". If it's there, the app can use the internet.

Also read the Data safety section on Google Play. It shows what the developer says the app collects and shares.

What "offline" should really mean

A truly offline markdown viewer:

  • Doesn't request network access at all
  • Has no ads (ads need the internet)
  • Has no analytics or crash-reporting SDKs
  • Doesn't need an account
  • Renders everything, including math and diagrams, with code bundled in the app instead of loading it from the web

Good habits for private notes

  • Keep sensitive notes in local storage instead of shared folders
  • Convert to PDF on the device instead of using online converters. See markdown to PDF on Android.
  • Check permissions again after big app updates
